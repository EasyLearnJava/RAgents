Hello World Agent Step 1 · rules only. Say "Hello" and it says "Hello World".
What you learn
  • An agent can be one plain function: respond(message) takes your text and returns a reply
  • How a page reacts to Send: the form's "submit" event runs onSend(e)
  • Rules are fast and predictable, but they only know what you wrote down
How a message travels
  1. Send (or Enter): the browser fires a "submit" event on the form, and onSend(e) runs.
  2. onSend(e) in index.html reads the text, puts your bubble on the page with add(text, "you") (unless it's empty or only spaces) and calls respond(text).
  3. respond() in agent.js trims the text and goes down its rules in order: empty → a hint; starts with hello, hi or hey → "Hello World"; anything else → a nudge.
  4. add(reply, "bot") puts the reply on the page, and the browser draws it in its next frame.

All of it runs in your browser. respond() usually takes less time than the browser's clock can measure; most of the wait until you see the reply is the browser's next frame (a 60 Hz screen draws one every 16.7 ms). "Where the time went", under the street view's tracker, shows your last message's real times.

What leaves your browser
  • Once, when the page opens: your browser downloads the page's files from the server: index.html, style.css, agent.js, and the extras' scripts (extras/*.js, /lib/town.js, /lib/flow.js).
  • For each message: nothing. There's no request; your text stays in this tab.
  • Check it: press F12 → Network and send a message: no new row appears. The street view's server panel counts the page's requests since your first message: it stays at 0.
Limits
  • Only greetings: hello, hi or hey at the start, in any case ("Hi there", "HEY!"). Anything else, even "helloooo" or "say hello", gets the same nudge.
  • No memory: each message is answered on its own; earlier ones make no difference.
  • It can't understand anything else: step 2 sends your message to a real model.
Safety
  • No script injection: messages are shown with textContent, so typing HTML or a <script> tag shows it as plain text; it never runs.
  • No keys or secrets: there's no model and no account, so the code has nothing to steal.
  • Nothing to protect: nothing is sent or stored. Close the tab and the chat is gone.
